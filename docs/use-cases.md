# What people use Rapport for

Rapport is a general voice library, so it fits anywhere people talk more than they type. These are the situations it was
built around, with the pain each one starts from. If yours isn't here, tell us in Discussions.

## Meetings without a bot

**Pain:** meeting bots announce themselves, sit in the participant list, and upload everything to someone's cloud.
**With Rapport:** record from your AirPods or the Mac mic, or pull the transcript from Granola or Notion afterwards.
Speakers are recognized across meetings, so "Speaker 2" becomes Priya once and stays Priya. Summaries follow your template.

## Interviews and journalism

**Pain:** hours of interview audio on a recorder, transcription services that cost per minute and keep a copy.
**With Rapport:** plug in the DJI Mic or Zoom recorder, walk away, come back to a speaker-labelled transcript. Search every
interview you've ever done for a phrase and jump to the second it was said. Nothing leaves your machine, which matters
for sources.

## User research and customer calls

**Pain:** dozens of calls, insights buried, the same customer across three sessions with no thread between them.
**With Rapport:** every participant is a person with a page: which sessions they were in, how long they spoke, what they said.
Search "pricing" across all sessions. Summaries with action items per call.

## Founders, sales and account management

**Pain:** a promise made on a call is forgotten by the next one.
**With Rapport:** the call is transcribed, the action items are in the summary, and the person's page keeps everything they've
told you. People memory and reminders (on the roadmap) turn "Sam mentioned a marathon in October" into a nudge in October.

## Lectures, seminars and study

**Pain:** two hours of lecture, no way to find the ten minutes that matter.
**With Rapport:** record on a phone or laptop, search by concept, listen back with pauses skipped, export the transcript
to your notes. Whisper handles 100+ languages and technical vocabulary better than most dictation apps.

## Therapists, coaches, doctors, lawyers

**Pain:** you can't send client audio to a cloud transcription service, so you don't transcribe at all.
**With Rapport:** everything stays on the Mac, in a folder you control, with no account. Transcripts help write notes;
the audio never goes anywhere. (Check your professional obligations; Rapport doesn't change them, it just removes the upload.)

## Voice memos and thinking out loud

**Pain:** ideas recorded on a walk die in Voice Memos, untitled and unsearchable.
**With Rapport:** memos from iPhone and Apple Watch arrive through iCloud, get transcribed, summarized and searchable by dinner.

## Podcasters and creators

**Pain:** finding the good take in three hours of raw audio.
**With Rapport:** the speaker lane shows who talked when; search finds the quote; condensed export removes the pauses.
The DJI Mic import was the first thing Rapport did.

## Field notes, research and oral history

**Pain:** recordings from the field with several voices and no time to log them.
**With Rapport:** batch import from a card, automatic speaker separation, per-person pages across sessions.

## Personal journal and memory

**Pain:** you want a record of what people told you and when, without building a CRM.
**With Rapport:** the People page is that record, built from conversations. People memory (roadmap) adds the details
people share, filed under the person, with reminders that know why.

## Accessibility and language learning

**Pain:** following fast speech, or reviewing your own pronunciation.
**With Rapport:** word-level highlighting during playback, adjustable speed, transcripts in the original language.

## Developers and tinkerers

**Pain:** you want your own voice data in your own tools.
**With Rapport:** everything is plain files and SQLite, the API is local HTTP, and the MCP server lets any LLM read
and annotate your library. See [developers.md](developers.md).
